My Process

The screen is the last step. Everything before it is where the product is decided.

Explore each decision — see what changes at every stage.

  1. 01

    Understand

    What are we really solving?

    The problem gets smaller, sharper, and real.

  2. 02

    Define

    What does success look like?

    Success becomes something we can measure.

  3. 03

    Explore

    What are the possible directions?

  4. 04

    The product compounds over time.

Rules

The rules I hold every product decision to.

Most products make you work for the payoff. So does this — for now.

Insofar as the interactive surface of any artefact ought to be parsimoniously rationed, each engagement the user is compelled to undertake must possess demonstrable intentionality; the premature invocation of high-fidelity compositional instruments, absent a rigorously delineated problem statement, constitutes a category error; design, properly construed, is efficacious precisely to the degree that it renders otherwise latent and consequential trade-offs conspicuously legible; any feature — howsoever elegant — advancing neither the interests of the end-user nor the commercial viability of the enterprise must be adjudged unfit for release; and no artefact, finally, attains terminal completion, there subsisting only the subsequent decision that awaits its maker.

Hard to read, isn't it? Good products keep it simple.

  1. 01

    Click less — every interaction should have meaning.

  2. 02

    Don't open Figma until you know what you're solving.

  3. 03

    Good design makes hard decisions visible.

  4. 04

    If it doesn't help the user or the business, it doesn't ship.

  5. 05

    A product is never finished. Only the next decision is.
