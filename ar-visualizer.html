AR Product Visualizer

Turning physical discovery into an interactive product experience.

Scroll — this is how I thought about it

01 — The observation

Curiosity starts before anyone opens an app.

A product on a shelf. A package. A printed advertisement. A display. Each one can catch someone's attention — but each one can only carry so much information.

So the next question is always: “Can I see more?”

Today, answering it means leaving: search, find the product, hunt for the information. The curiosity has to survive the trip.

See product → Search online → Find it → Look for information

What if the product could come alive?

The digital layer appears where the curiosity started.

03 — The pattern

It isn't about one product.

Treating AR as a one-off visual effect would have been the smaller idea. The interesting part is the interaction pattern underneath — which holds wherever a physical thing meets a curious person.

  • Packaging See product → Activate → Explore
  • Print advertising See advertisement → Activate → Watch
  • Retail See product → Activate → Understand
  • Events See display → Activate → Experience
  • Demonstrations See product → Activate → Learn

The context changes. The experience doesn't.

Physical touchpoint → digital experience.

04 — The design question

The question was never “what can AR do?”

Where does the product experience actually begin?

We tend to assume it begins when someone opens an app or visits a site. But it begins earlier — when they notice a package, walk past a display, or pick the thing up. That moment could be the first interaction rather than the one before it.

The real opportunity isn't making objects more entertaining. It is shortening the distance between “this caught my attention” and “now I understand it.”

05 — What this exploration represents

A package can become an interface.

This wasn't an exercise in adding AR to a product. It was an exploration of what happens when the boundary between a physical product and a digital product experience stops being rigid.

  • A package can become an interface.
  • An advertisement can become an interaction.
  • A display can become a demonstration.

The screen doesn't always have to be the starting point.

Don't make curiosity leave the physical world to find the digital experience.

See → Activate → Experience → Understand. The technology enables the interaction; the interaction is the product idea.
