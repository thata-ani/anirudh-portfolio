The context

Agriculture is decision-driven.

What to plant. When to act. Which product to trust. A farmer's season is a chain of decisions — but the information behind those decisions is fragmented, spread across sources that don't connect.

At the same time, Syngenta wanted to strengthen its relationship with growers beyond the point of purchase. That created a dual need:

For the farmer

Help farmers make better everyday farming decisions.

+

For Syngenta

Create a digital ecosystem that builds grower relationships beyond purchases.

Neither need could be an afterthought of the other. The product had to serve both — at the same time.

The challenge

Two objectives that couldn't compromise each other.

If the product only served the farmer, it wouldn't sustain the business. If it only served the business, farmers wouldn't use it.

What farmers needed

  • Reduce information fragmentation
  • Improve decision-making
  • Timely recommendations
  • Access to expertise
  • Simplify farm management

What the business needed

  • Engagement
  • Awareness
  • Loyalty
  • Demand generation
  • A scalable ecosystem

The larger question

How do we help farmers solve daily farming problems while creating long-term engagement opportunities?

What I was really solving

One product couldn't be one fixed experience.

Cropwise Grower serves markets across AMEA — and those markets have different crops, different practices, and different needs. I couldn't design one fixed experience and ship it everywhere. Every design decision had to answer three connected questions:

Cropwise Grower

What does the farmer need?

What does this market need?

How can the product connect the two?

The answers changed by market. The questions never did.

The insight

Farmers don't search for features. Farmers search for answers.

"I need Crop Doctor"

"Why are my leaves turning yellow?"

"I need a weather service"

"Is today the right day to spray?"

"I need a seed catalogue"

"What should I plant this season?"

That reframing changed the approach — from designing a list of features to designing connected services around the questions farmers actually ask.

From services to an ecosystem

Each service solves a different problem.

The value increases when they work together — one service's answer becomes the next service's starting point.

Weather Advisory Crop Doctor Experts Crop Protection Seeds Services Loyalty

Not eight features on a menu — one connected ecosystem.

The product work

Six services, one pattern of thinking.

Every service follows the same logic: a farmer problem, a product idea, an experience flow — and the point where farmer value connects to business value.

Service 01

Crop Doctor

ProblemWhen a crop shows symptoms, the farmer needs to know what's wrong — fast. Waiting for whoever happens to be available costs the crop time.

IdeaTake the farmer from symptom to solution in one connected flow — diagnosis first, then a recommendation, then discovery of what can fix it.

Experience flow — Crop Doctor
Symptom Diagnosis Recommendation Solution discovery

Farmer value

A faster, more confident answer to "what's wrong with my crop?"

Business connection

The recommendation naturally introduces relevant crop protection products — solution discovery, not advertising.

What I learned

The biggest lesson wasn't about features. It was about ecosystem thinking.

Designing an individual service well is craft. Designing how services strengthen each other — so that helping a farmer and growing the business become the same motion — is product thinking.

  • A service is useful alone. An ecosystem is valuable together.
  • Business value follows farmer value — never the other way around.
  • One product can serve many markets if the emphasis adapts, not the foundation.

Farmer problem → Product decision → Experience → Ecosystem → Business value.

Cropwise Grower helps farmers make better decisions — and that help is the demand-generation model.

The farmer gets an answer. The business earns a relationship. The same design serves both.
