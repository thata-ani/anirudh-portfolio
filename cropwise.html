The context

Agriculture is decision-driven.

What to plant. When to act. Which product to trust. A farmer's season is a chain of decisions — but the information behind those decisions is fragmented, spread across sources that don't connect.

At the same time, Syngenta wanted to strengthen its relationship with growers beyond the point of purchase. That created a dual need:

For the farmer

Help farmers make better everyday farming decisions.

+

For Syngenta

Create a digital ecosystem that builds grower relationships beyond purchases.

Neither need could be an afterthought of the other. The product had to serve both — at the same time.

The challenge

Two objectives that couldn't compromise each other.

If the product only served the farmer, it wouldn't sustain the business. If it only served the business, farmers wouldn't use it.

What farmers needed

  • Reduce information fragmentation
  • Improve decision-making
  • Timely recommendations
  • Access to expertise
  • Simplify farm management

What the business needed

  • Engagement
  • Awareness
  • Loyalty
  • Demand generation
  • A scalable ecosystem

The larger question

How do we help farmers solve daily farming problems while creating long-term engagement opportunities?

What I was really solving

One product couldn't be one fixed experience.

Cropwise Grower serves markets across AMEA — and those markets have different crops, different practices, and different needs. I couldn't design one fixed experience and ship it everywhere. Every design decision had to answer three connected questions:

Cropwise Grower

What does the farmer need?

What does this market need?

How can the product connect the two?

The answers changed by market. The questions never did.

The insight

Farmers don't search for features. Farmers search for answers.

"I need Crop Doctor"

"Why are my leaves turning yellow?"

"I need a weather service"

"Is today the right day to spray?"

"I need a seed catalogue"

"What should I plant this season?"

That reframing changed the approach — from designing a list of features to designing connected services around the questions farmers actually ask.

From services to an ecosystem

Each service solves a different problem.

The value increases when they work together — one service's answer becomes the next service's starting point.

Crop Calendar Crop Doctor Crop Protection Retailers Seeds Loyalty

Not six features on a menu — one connected ecosystem.

The product work

Six business needs. Six ways to create farmer value.

Cropwise carried a different business objective in each of its services. My role was to look past the requirement itself — because a requirement answered literally becomes a screen, and a requirement understood becomes an experience.

The four questions I asked of every service

What does the business need?

Why would the farmer care?

What experience can make that valuable?

How can that experience support the wider ecosystem?

Every service below follows that same path — business need, what I saw, the product direction it led to, and where farmer value meets business value.

01

Enroll Crop + Crop Calendar

The advisory foundation, and a personalised crop journey.

Business needCropwise had to stay relevant across the whole crop lifecycle — not only in the moments when something went wrong. It also needed enough crop context to make its advisory genuinely personal.

What I sawGeneric information is noise. But if the product knows the crop, the variety, the sowing date and the location, it knows where that farmer stands in their season — and that context is what everything else can personalise against.

Product direction Enroll once. Stay relevant for the whole season.

Experience flow — Crop Calendar
Crop context Current stage What's coming What to do

Farmer value

No more searching through generic advice. The product can answer the only question that matters: "what applies to my crop, at this point in my season?"

Demand-generation connection

The business gets something more valuable than another content surface — a reason to stay relevant all season. Crop context → personalised guidance → recurring engagement → relevant solution.

This made personalisation the ecosystem's foundation rather than an isolated advisory feature.

02

Crop Doctor

A digital entry point built around the problem, not the portfolio.

Business needCreate a meaningful digital entry point around crop problems, and connect farmers with relevant crop-protection solutions.

What I sawNo farmer ever opens an app thinking "show me crop-protection products." They open it thinking "something is wrong with my crop." That one reframing changes what the product opportunity actually is.

Product direction Start with the farmer's problem, and carry them to action without a second search.

Experience flow — Crop Doctor
Symptom Diagnosis Recommendation Relevant solution

Farmer value

A faster path from "what's wrong?" to "what should I do?" — moving from uncertainty to action in one flow.

Demand-generation connection

Once the farmer understands the problem, a relevant solution has a legitimate place in the journey. Not product → promotion, but problem → help → understanding → relevant solution.

The business objective is achieved by solving the farmer's problem, not by interrupting it.

03

Crop Protection

Discovery moved out of the catalogue and into the decision.

Business needImprove awareness and adoption of crop-protection solutions.

What I sawA catalogue doesn't help anybody decide. The farmer's question is contextual — "what is right for my crop, in my situation?" — and a list of products structurally cannot answer it.

Product direction A recommendation should make sense because of why it is being shown, not merely because it exists.

Experience flow — Crop Protection
Farmer context Relevant recommendation Product understanding Confident choice

Farmer value

Help understanding the one solution that applies, instead of decoding a long product list alone.

Demand-generation connection

The product becomes discoverable at the moment the farmer has a genuine reason to consider it: need → recommendation → understanding → adoption.

The commercial objective becomes part of the experience rather than something layered on top of it.

04

Seed Recommendations

Entering the journey before the season begins.

Business needEngage growers earlier in the crop journey, and create opportunities around seed consideration.

What I sawSeed decisions are made long before crop-management problems appear. If the product only becomes useful once something has gone wrong, the relationship has already started too late.

Product direction Meet the farmer while they are still planning — helping them decide, not presenting a portfolio.

Experience flow — Seed Recommendations
Season planning Options Comparison Decision

Farmer value

Confidence at a decision that shapes the entire season: "what should I consider planting this year?"

Demand-generation connection

Rather than building awareness in isolation, Cropwise creates the context in which the relevant portfolio naturally enters consideration: planning → consideration → decision.

05

Loyalty

Making the purchase another way in, not the way out.

Business needExtend the relationship beyond the purchase, and encourage continued engagement.

What I sawA traditional relationship ends at purchase → leave. But a purchase is also the one moment we know a farmer has acted — which makes it the strongest possible entry point back into the ecosystem.

Product direction Turn the transaction into an ongoing relationship — the reward is a reason to stay connected, not just an incentive.

Experience flow — Loyalty
Purchase Rewards Return Engage

Farmer value

Recognition, and tangible value returned for participation they were already giving.

Demand-generation connection

A mechanism for retention → repeat engagement → loyalty. The relationship stops resetting itself after every purchase.

06

Nearby Retailers

Closing the gap between recommendation and action.

Business needA digital recommendation alone creates no business impact. A farmer can identify the problem, receive guidance and understand the right solution — and the journey still breaks if they don't know where to buy it, which retailer is nearby, or whether they can get it quickly.

What I sawThe journey doesn't end when the product names a solution. The real question is "now that I know what I need, how do I get it?" Between recommendation and purchase sat an access gap that nothing in the product addressed.

Problem identification Recommendation Purchase access gap

Product direction Connect the digital experience to the physical retailer network, so the farmer can reach action without leaving the ecosystem.

Experience flow — Nearby Retailers
Need Relevant solution Nearby retailer Purchase

Farmer value

Faster action, far less time spent hunting for an agri store or relying on word of mouth — and the confidence of buying genuine product through an authorised channel.

Demand-generation connection

Most digital products stop at awareness → recommendation. Here the chain continues through retailer discovery to purchase — and the opportunity exists only because the farmer was helped first.

The product decision Don't stop at telling farmers what they need. Help them reach the next step.

Connecting farmer value with business value

Help first. Demand follows.

The business objective was never really "how do we promote more products?" The more useful design question was "what would make the farmer genuinely value this interaction?" — and only then do the two objectives meet.

  1. Business need
  2. Farmer problem
  3. Product decision
  4. Useful experience
  5. Farmer value
  6. Engagement and trust
  7. Relevant business opportunity

That is the pattern behind all six services. I wasn't designing business requirements into screens — I was translating business objectives into experiences farmers had a reason to use. When that value is genuine, demand generation becomes a consequence of the experience rather than an interruption to it.

For Syngenta

What I learned

The biggest lesson wasn't about features. It was about ecosystem thinking.

Designing an individual service well is craft. Designing how services strengthen each other — so that helping a farmer and growing the business become the same motion — is product thinking.

  • A service is useful alone. An ecosystem is valuable together.
  • Business value follows farmer value — never the other way around.
  • One product can serve many markets if the emphasis adapts, not the foundation.

Farmer problem → Product decision → Experience → Ecosystem → Business value.

Cropwise Grower helps farmers make better decisions — and that help is the demand-generation model.

The farmer gets an answer. The business earns a relationship. The same design serves both.
